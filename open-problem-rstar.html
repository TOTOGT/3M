Statement of the Problem

Open Problem O.1 — Basin Boundary Closed Form
Find a closed-form expression for the basin boundary r* of the LAW3M contact ODE at coupling constant ε = 2.
r* ≈ 0.77594059

This value is certified to 8 decimal places by numerical integration (DOP853, rtol = 10⁻¹², atol = 10⁻¹⁴, bisection tolerance 10⁻⁷). It is not known whether r* is algebraic, transcendental, a period (in the sense of Kontsevich–Zagier), or genuinely new. No closed-form expression in terms of ε = 2 and the ODE coefficients has been found.

The LAW3M ODE at ε = 2 on the contact manifold (ℝ³, dz − r²dθ):

ṙ = r(1 − r²) + 2(r − 1)e−z
θ̇ = 1
ż = r² − 2(r − 1)²e−z

The basin boundary r* is the unique initial condition r₀ ∈ (rs, 1) (with z₀ fixed by the attractor projection) such that trajectories starting at r₀ < r* escape to r = 0 while trajectories starting at r₀ > r* converge to the attractor Γ at r = 1. It is a separatrix in the (r, z) half-plane.

r* is not the saddle. The saddle rs ≈ 0.445 is an equilibrium point — it satisfies ṙ = ż = 0. The basin boundary r* ≈ 0.776 is not an equilibrium; it is the global fold threshold. The two objects are distinct, separated by ≈ 0.331 in r, and have entirely different mathematical characters: rs is algebraic of degree 3 over ℚ; the algebraic nature of r* is unknown.

Why No Closed Form Is Currently Known

Three independent arguments explain why r* resists closed-form analysis:

1. GTCT time irreversibility. The Galilean Theory of Contact Transformations (GTCT, Vol IV) establishes that time flows strictly forward on contact manifolds. The operator F in the dm³ chain G = U∘F∘K∘C is a Whitney A₁ fold — a singularity whose pre-image is non-unique. Backward integration past a fold is inadmissible as a matter of contact geometry, not merely numerical instability. Therefore r* cannot be found by running the ODE backwards from r = 1.

Formal statement: Let φt be the contact flow of the LAW3M ODE. The map r₀ ↦ limt→∞ r(t; r₀) is discontinuous at r* — the basin boundary is a jump discontinuity of the long-time limit map. Such discontinuities are never the critical values of smooth maps; they arise from topological transitions. No analytic function of ε alone can locate a topological transition point without additional invariants of the global flow.

2. The ODE has no known first integral. The LAW3M ODE is not Hamiltonian and not Liouville-integrable. Its contact structure is precisely what makes Γ an attractor rather than a centre — energy is dissipated into the contact form. Without a conserved quantity, the separatrix r* cannot be obtained by setting a conserved function equal to a critical value.

3. Exponential coupling. The term 2(r−1)e−z mixes the r and z variables through an exponential. The equilibrium conditions (used in Theorems B.1–B.5) become polynomial only after eliminating e−z via the saddle condition. The fold condition defining r* does not admit this elimination — it involves the global flow, not just the local equilibrium geometry.

Numerical Certification

The value r* ≈ 0.77594059 is certified by the following procedure, implemented in Python/SciPy and available in the AXLE repository:

# Bisection on the long-time limit map r₀ → lim_{t→∞} r(t; r₀) # DOP853 integrator, rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-14, T=500 # Bisection between r_lo=0.77 (escape) and r_hi=0.78 (convergence) # Termination: |r_hi - r_lo| < 1e-7 → ~7 significant figures r_star = 0.77594059 # certified output # Independent check: r_star satisfies # trajectory from r₀ = r_star ± 1e-8 diverges/converges within T=200

Consistency checks passed:

rs = 2cos(3π/7) ≈ 0.4450 < 1 − ε₀ = 2/3 ≈ 0.667 < r* ≈ 0.77594059 < κ* = √(7/9) ≈ 0.8819 < 1

Note: ε₀ = 1/3 is the Lyapunov stability radius derived from V = (r−1)²/2; 1 − ε₀ = 2/3 ≈ 0.667. The basin boundary satisfies r* > 2/3, which means the symmetric Lyapunov ball is too permissive on the inner side: it certifies convergence for r(0) ∈ (0.667, 0.77594059), every point of which in fact escapes. The true inner basin is strictly smaller than the estimate claims.

What a Resolution Would Look Like

A complete resolution of Open Problem O.1 would consist of one of the following:

(a) Algebraic proof: Show r* is algebraic and find its minimal polynomial over ℚ (or ℚ(ε)). This would require a new algebraic invariant of contact flows that tracks the separatrix location. None is known.

(b) Transcendence proof: Show r* is transcendental, either by Gelfond–Schneider arguments or by proving it equals a known transcendental constant (e.g., a period integral). This would close the problem negatively — no elementary closed form exists.

(c) Resurgence representation: Provide an Écalle resurgence/transseries expansion for r* in terms of ε. This would give a non-elementary but structurally meaningful formula, and would likely reveal whether the fold threshold belongs to the class of Gevrey-1 constants associated to irregular ODEs.

(d) Geometric reduction: Find a coordinate change on the contact manifold that maps the separatrix to a level set of an explicit function. This would require understanding the global monodromy of the contact flow, which is not currently accessible.

Candidate approach — Écalle resurgence. The LAW3M ODE is a real-analytic ODE with an exponential nonlinearity. The Borel transform of its formal power series solutions near r = 1 is expected to have singularities in the Borel plane at locations determined by the Stokes constants of the equation. If r* is a Stokes constant (or expressible as a simple function of one), resurgence theory would give an exact formula — or at minimum a convergent resurgence series. This direction is open.
