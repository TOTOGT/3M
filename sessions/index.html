Helical Attractors on Contact 3-Manifolds

Three 60-minute sessions: from the contact form that forces a helix, through the theorem and its asymmetric basin, to the Lean 4 formalisation and its one open obligation.
Pre-req: undergraduate ODE | Total: 3 hours | UFRN, Natal-RN
The Three Sessions

Course Path

Session S1 · 60 min · lecture + live simulator
Contact Geometry & the dm³ System
The arena: a contact 3-manifold, the Reeb field, and why non-integrability turns a periodic orbit into a helix. Introduces the dm³ ODE and the coupling ε(r−1)e−z.
Session S2 · 60 min · lecture + numerical demo
Theorem 2.1 & the Asymmetric Basin
Linearisation gives the eigenvalue −2. The Lyapunov estimate gives a symmetric ball |r−1| < 1/3. DOP853 says the real inner edge is r⋆ = 0.77594059. The gap is the course.
Session S3 · 60 min · code walk-through
Lean 4 Skeleton & AXLE Issue #12
Formalising Theorem 2.1 in Mathlib 4. What is proved, what is sorry, what is axiom — and why the Lipschitz constant on the coupling is the one open door.

On the inner boundary. The basin of Γ is not symmetric about r = 1. The Lyapunov estimate certifies |r−1| < 1/3, i.e. r(0) ∈ (2/3, 4/3), but the true inner edge sits above 2/3, at the certified value r⋆ = 0.77594059 — so the estimate misclassifies every r(0) between 0.667 and 0.776 as convergent when in fact it escapes. The outer side has no boundary at all in the tested range. Reproduce with certify_rstar.py; the hierarchy ε₀ = 1/3 < 2/3 < r⋆ < κ⋆ = √(7/9) < 1 is checked by the script.
