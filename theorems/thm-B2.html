Statement

Theorem B.2 — Fundamental Identity
At the saddle rs = 2cos(3π/7):
(1 + rs − rs²)² = 2 − rs

This identity connects the trace of the Jacobian at the saddle (Theorem B.3) to the saddle value rs itself. It is not an approximation — it is an exact polynomial identity that holds because rs satisfies the cubic of Theorem B.1.

Proof

Let t = 1 + rs − rs². We compute t² directly:

t² = (1 + rs − rs²)² = 1 + rs² + rs⁴ + 2rs − 2rs² − 2rs³

Use the minimal polynomial rs³ = rs² + 2rs − 1 to reduce powers ≥ 3:

rs³ = rs² + 2rs − 1
rs⁴ = rs · rs³ = rs(rs² + 2rs − 1) = rs³ + 2rs² − rs = (rs²+2rs−1)+2rs²−rs = 3rs²+rs−1

Substituting back:

t² = 1 + rs² + (3rs²+rs−1) + 2rs − 2rs² − 2(rs²+2rs−1)
= 1 + rs² + 3rs² + rs − 1 + 2rs − 2rs² − 2rs² − 4rs + 2
= (1 − 1 + 2) + (1 + 2 − 4)rs + (1 + 3 − 2 − 2)rs²
= 2 − rs + 0 · rs²
t² = 2 − rs   ★ □

Literature Gap Closed

Gap closed: The identity (1+rs−rs²)² = 2−rs is the bridge between the Jacobian trace (a linear expression in rs) and the square-root form needed to identify it with 2cos(2π/7). Without this identity, Theorem B.3 cannot be stated as a clean cosine formula — it remains an implicit algebraic expression.

No prior work in the contact-ODE literature identified this intermediate identity or its role in connecting saddle geometry to 7th-root-of-unity structure.

Physical Consequence

Algebraic coherence: The identity 2−rs = tr(J)² = (2cos(2π/7))² is the reason the system's stability rate is a perfect cosine — not an accident but a consequence of the cubic's minimal polynomial. It means the rotating energy system's divergence at the saddle is constrained by the same 7-fold symmetry that governs the attractor structure.
